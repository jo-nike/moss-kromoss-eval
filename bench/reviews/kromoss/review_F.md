## Failure modes

1. **The light source lands; its consequences don't.** The lit side was right in all 7 images that named one (F001, F002, F010, F011, F013, F024, F050). But backlit subjects stay front-lit (F006, F012), shadows are missing (F016, F025, F039) or point toward the sun (F027), and caustics and edge highlights drop (F029, F049).
2. **Named techniques and gear become literal objects.** "Butterfly lighting" gave a butterfly-wing shadow plus an in-frame reflector plate (F004); heat shimmer became a stacked copy of the truck and the mirage a lake (F039). Rembrandt (F001) and split (F002) worked, so this rests on 2-3 images.
3. **Diagonal and relative directions get mirrored.** The snow falls upper right to lower left (F038), and the umbrella shadows point toward the sun (F027). The dog is on the girl's left, not her right (F006).
4. **Contact details drop out:** the rake is fused with the torch (F024), the hands do not hold the leaf (F047), and the strawberry sits on a pink cylinder (F049).
5. **Small anatomy artifacts:** an eye-like blob on a collar (F010), an oversized nose (F023), and a kingfisher's head hidden behind its wing (F035). Hands were clean throughout (F008, F009, F024, F047).

## What worked

- **Materials:** caustics (F014), ice (F046), soap film (F048), velvet (F044), wet asphalt (F045), the sneaker (F030), brushed and polished steel (F028, F043).
- **Focus placement:** the third jar of six is the sharp one (F017). The tilt-shift (F021), panning (F019) and macro (F020) shots all landed.

## What correlated with success

- **Length barely mattered.** Prompts of 30 words or fewer: 4.33 (n=6). Prompts of 31-200 words: 4.17 (n=40). Prompts over 200 words: 3.75 (n=4). The long 3s (F006, F049) failed on physics, while F028 scored 5 at 364 words.
- **Looks beat causes.** Named looks landed (infrared, cross-processing, double exposure, blue hour, gels). Effects the model must derive from light placement missed (silhouettes, shadow direction, caustics).

## Prompting advice

1. Describe the visible result, not the setup: "face in soft shadow, hair glowing", not just "sun behind her" (untested; evidence F006, F012).
2. Avoid technique or gear names that are also object names (butterfly, reflector). Describe the shadow shape instead.
3. Don't rely on diagonal or shadow directions. Run several seeds when they matter.
4. State contact explicitly ("strawberry half-sunk in the milk") and still expect misses.
5. Short quoted text is safe, even when small. This rests on only 4 cases.
6. Long, dense prompts are fine for material showcases. F028, F030, F046 and F048 were among the best images.
